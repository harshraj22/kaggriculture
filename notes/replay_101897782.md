# Replay review — episode 101897782, us 21,620 vs truebelief 86,511

Our first ranked submission (`melon_farm`, rating 439, rank 5283). Not a bug:
every episode COMPLETED, no timeouts, and we banked roughly what we score
locally. We were simply out-earned 4x.

## The finding that overturns our whole model

**We sold the same volume and earned a quarter as much.**

    us          741 units  ->  24,013
    truebelief  764 units  -> 102,110

It is not production. It is **price per unit**, and price is set by market
inventory relative to `I0`. Shops CONSUME products, draining inventory *below*
`I0`, which pushes price *above* base. Measured from this replay:

    product      base      day 5        day 15       day 25
    MILK          160   9983 -> 196   9913 -> 241   9885 -> 253
    WOOL          200   9995 -> 215   9913 -> 238   9833 -> 244
    STRAWBERRY    120   9983 -> 155   9909 -> 200   9707 -> 264
    MELON         250   9995 -> 266  10087 -> 174  10137 ->  62
    WHEAT          25  10013 ->  23  10207 ->  21  10231 ->  20

Strawberry more than doubled over the season. Melon collapsed to a quarter.
The difference is entirely whether a shop drains that market.

**This corrects an earlier analysis in this project.** We computed "static
ceilings" by dumping until the price floors, and concluded EGG (149k) and WHEAT
(75k) had the most headroom. That models only the glut side and is the wrong
frame: the winning play is to sell MODEST volumes into markets shops keep
scarce, not large volumes into any market. We sold 631 wheat at an average of
20.4 — below its base of 25 — pushing our own price down all game.

## Completed review — tempo, labour, land, action mix

### The economic engine we are missing

**Structures are FREE.** `BUILD_PASTURE` / `BUILD_COOP` set the tile kind with no
cost check in the engine — the only capital is the animal itself.

    animal capital (9 COW + 5 SHEEP orders)    6,100
    feed bought (171 WHEAT @ ~20)              3,420
    animal revenue (milk + wool + fertilizer) 62,552
    return on spend                              6.6x

Note what that arbitrage is: **buy wheat at ~20, feed it to a cow, sell milk at
~250.** They bought 171 wheat as feed rather than growing all of it, because
wheat is the cheapest thing on the board and the conversion ratio is absurd.

### Tempo — they invest first, we spend first

    day    them                                      us
    0-9    25 tiles, 5 hands, 4 pastures,            50 tiles, 8 hands,
           bank ~900 the whole time                  bank 584-2400
    10     buys 2nd quadrant (h20), first cow        (bought land day 0, hour 1)
    15     11 pastures, 7 cows, 4 sheep, 9 hands     14 wheat + 10 melon, 17 empty
    20-29  bank 27k -> 63k -> 86k                    bank 18k -> 21k

They hold near-zero bank for a third of the season while the herd is assembled,
then compound. We buy land in hour 1 and then grow linearly and stall.

### Labour and action mix

    unit-turns   movement  water  pass  harvest  feed/care/pickup/collect  plant
    us   5392       62%     25%    4%     3%              0%               5%
    them 5945       54%     14%   14%     4%             11%               2%

We are a watering treadmill: a quarter of every turn spent watering, nearly
two-thirds walking between tiles. They spend 11% of turns on the animal loop
(FEED / CARE / PICKUP / COLLECT_FERTILIZER) and **deliberately idle 14%** rather
than make low-value moves.

`PICKUP` matters mechanically: `FEED` consumes wheat from the *acting unit's own
inventory*, so wheat must be carried from the shed to the animal.

Hiring is NOT one of our problems: 8/day, 1,620 for the season (they spent 3,474
and scaled 5 -> 11 with income). The "36 hires/day" in the raw order counts is
redundant order *quantities*, not fills — though those redundant orders do eat
into the 10-orders-per-turn cap.

### Land utilisation

We ran 14-21 empty tiles plus 4-11 weeds from day 6 onward — half the farm idle
while paying for 8 hands. They held **zero weeds all game** on a smaller farm.

## Ranked gaps, final

1. **No animals.** 61% of their revenue, 6.6x on capital, free structures.
2. **We glut the one market we produce most of.** 631 wheat at 20.4 avg, below
   its base of 25. Selling less would pay more per unit, and the surplus is feed.
3. **Buy-to-feed arbitrage unused.** Wheat at 20 -> milk at 250.
4. **Half the land idle** while we pay for the labour to work it.
5. **Land bought on hour 1** instead of once income supports it.
6. **Melon is the worst market to build around** — no shop demand, so the glut
   never clears; two farms dumping took it 266 -> 62.
